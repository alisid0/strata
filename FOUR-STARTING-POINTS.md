# FOUR-STARTING-POINTS.md — the four learning paths (detailed)

Qubix has four "Ground Zero" learning paths. Each begins from a **smallest
thing** and grows outward with nothing assumed — every new idea earns its place
from the one before it. They are four staircases up the same building: a user
climbs one, switches at shared landings, or wanders freely. Nothing is
mandatory; the paths are suggestions, not rails.

This file restores the **act-by-act progressions** we designed. Each path is a
sequence of numbered steps (about one BB each) grouped into acts. Two recurring
mechanics:

- **Honest debts.** A path openly flags a question it cannot yet answer
  ("classical physics has no answer here — flag planted"), then pays it off
  acts later. The user can see the books.
- **Cross-path checkpoints.** A step sometimes needs an idea from another path.
  That is a soft suggestion ("best after Line, step 15"), never a lock, and a
  payoff when the other path is already done ("already N/M read").

Status: LINE (through matrices), ATOM (through bonding), and UNIT (through the
motion handoff) were walked step-by-step. BIT has its act skeleton. Forks left
open are marked with a triangle.

## 1. THE LINE — the smallest piece of space / abstraction

### Act 1 — The plane, from a single line
1. **What a line is** — everyday line, then geometric line (straight, no
   thickness), then Euclidean line (extends forever, no ends).
2. **Line vs ray vs segment** — two ends, one end, no ends. Quick test.
3. **Numbers on the line** — choose a zero, choose a step; every point gets a
   name.
4. **Types of numbers** — natural, whole, integers (the line goes left),
   rationals (the marks between). Quick test.
5. **The line goes both ways** — negatives; opposites as mirror points.
6. **Distance** — the gap between two marks; subtraction is measuring; distance
   is never negative.
7. **The line lies down** — we name it the x-axis.
8. **A second line stands up** — the y-axis; the plane; every point an (x, y)
   address.
9. **Units, mass, dimensions** — what measuring is; mass as the first measured
   quantity; length/mass/time as base ingredients. (This is where the UNIT path
   can merge in — see section 4.)
10. **A mass on the line** — a dot with mass at x; let it move, and 1D motion is
    born.
11. **Newton's three laws** — derived from watching the dot, not announced.
12. **The rectangle** — area as counting unit squares.
13. **The function** — a machine: number in, number out.

### Act 2 — Force makes the arrow (vectors)
14. **The second mass** — two dots push and pull; the third law appears as an
    observation.
15. **How hard? F = ma** — same push on different masses; force becomes a number
    because acceleration measures it.
16. **Direction is not a mood** — on the line, direction is a sign; admit it and
    the **arrow** is born. (The idea the ATOM path borrows at its charge act.)
17. **Arrows on the plane** — the diagonal push casts two shadows (components).
18. **Adding arrows** — tip to tail: *why 5 + 5 isn't always 10.* (This is
    BB #001.)
19. **The net force** — many arrows, one sum; stillness is arrows cancelling.
20. **The ever-present arrow** — weight; mass and weight split cleanly, using
    the units from step 9.

### Act 3 — The plane becomes programmable (matrices)
21. **The arrow gets an address** — an arrow from the origin IS its tip (3,4);
    points and arrows merge.
22. **Arrow arithmetic** — tip-to-tail addition equals adding coordinates.
23. **How long is an arrow?** — Pythagoras arrives because (3,4) demands a
    length; uses the rectangle from step 12.
24. **The machine that moves every arrow** — rotate, stretch, flip the plane;
    each is a function (step 13 returns, now eating arrows).
25. **The matrix** — any grid-respecting machine is fixed by where just two
    arrows land: four numbers. A matrix is a machine, compressed.
26. **Matrix times vector** — the rule derived, not memorised.
27. **Machines in sequence** — matrix multiplication; order matters.
28. **The machine that undoes** — inverses; the determinant as area-scaling;
    machines that squash the plane flat.

Act 4 fork (open): trigonometry (rotation needs an angle) · image
filters/pixels (a photo is a grid of numbers) · systems of equations (the
matrix as solver) · back to projectiles (velocity as an arrow, thrown).

## 2. THE ATOM — the smallest piece of matter

### Act 1 — How small can small be?
1. **The question** — is there a smallest thing, or can you divide forever?
   Posed straight, textbook-serious.
2. **Defining small** — "small" is a ratio; a ladder of analogies, rung to
   rung.
3. **The failure of intuition** — perception bottoms out; below that we trade
   senses for arithmetic. Numbers go where minds cannot.
4. **Avogadro's number** — any visible pinch holds a count beyond experience;
   the exchange rate between our scale and theirs.
5. **Atomic number** — one number fixes what an atom *is* (proton count).
   Identity as a count.
6. **Atomic mass** — why weight and ID disagree; isotopes; mass as average;
   grams-per-mole ties counting to weighing.
7. **Recipes** — compounds as fixed recipes; formulas as their notation.
8. **Rearrangement** — reactions destroy nothing; balancing is bookkeeping; the
   candle's wax leaves as gas.
9. **The album** — the periodic table, ordered by proton count. Its ordering is
   understood; its **shape** is not yet. (Debt planted.)
10. **The pivot** — some matter conducts, dissolved salt suddenly does;
    something charged must live inside and move. Turn toward electricity.

### Act 2 — The atom has insides (charge)
11. **The balloon trick** — rub a balloon; you manufacture a force by hand, and
    it is not gravity.
12. **Two flavours** — charge comes in exactly two kinds; like repels, opposite
    attracts — found with a party trick.
13. **Stolen, never made** — the balloon gains what the hair loses, exactly;
    charge is a thing that can move.
14. **The uncuttable has parts** — a negative piece rubs off, so the atom has
    insides (light negative electron, heavy positive remainder).
15. **Push without touching** — charges act across space; double distance,
    quarter force; and the push has **direction**. (Cross-path checkpoint: the
    arrow, LINE step 16.)
16. **Mostly nothing** — nearly all mass in a tiny nucleus; the atom is
    overwhelmingly empty space.
17. **Why doesn't it collapse?** — electrons should spiral in; classical physics
    has no answer. (Debt planted; later merges with the shell debt.)
18. **The album's secret, half-revealed** — species differ by a count; the
    table's ordering explained, its **shape** still owed.

### Act 3 — Charges on the march (current)
19. **The disloyal electron** — in metals, outer electrons drift in a shared
    sea; conductors vs insulators; explains the salt-water pivot.
20. **The march** — current is charge per second past a point; the electrons
    crawl but the *push* travels near light-speed (the light is instant).
21. **The pump** — a battery maintains a pressure difference (voltage); it does
    not make charge.
22. **The crowd resists** — resistance from bumping; narrow or long means more;
    a hotter wire resists more (debt: heat is jiggle, not yet earned).
23. **Ohm's bookkeeping** — V = IR by sliding values; a behaviour, not a
    commandment.
24. **The loop** — a circuit must be complete; series vs parallel; household
    wiring follows.
25. **The toll** — every bump surrenders energy, so wires heat and filaments
    glow; power is pressure times flow. (Debt: energy deserves its own act.)
26. **The twitch** — a compass needle turns near a live wire; nothing so far
    predicts it. Act ends mid-sentence.

### Act 4 — Why the columns rhyme (bonding)
27. **The rhyme** — sodium and potassium behave alike; columns rhyme chemically.
28. **Layers** — ionization-energy jumps reveal shells with fixed capacities
    (2, 8, 8); *why* is not yet explainable (joins the collapse debt).
29. **Only the outside counts** — same outer count means same behaviour; a row
    ends when a shell fills. **The table's shape is paid.** (Debt from step 9.)
30. **The contented ones** — a full shell is noble-gas silence; "wanting" is a
    metaphor for settling toward lower energy.
31. **Give and take** — ionic bonding is electrostatics doing chemistry; the
    salt-water pivot pays off in full.
32. **The share** — covalent bonding; the water recipe (2 H) becomes a
    *consequence* of shell counts.
33. **The sea, explained** — metallic bonding; step 19's disloyal electron was
    this all along.
34. **Shape** — shared pairs repel, so molecules have shapes; the bent water
    molecule is why it pries salt apart. Steps 10, 31, 32 close into a loop.

Act 5 fork (open): chase the twitch (magnetism) · fire (the energy act via
burning) · flame colours (light, the quantum debt) · everyday chemistry (acids
and reactions).

**Debts ledger after Act 4:** PAID — table's shape, salt-water, disloyal
electron. STILL OWED — why shells have capacities (quantum), energy's own act,
the compass twitch.

## 3. THE BIT — the smallest piece of information
*(Act skeleton designed; steps to be walked out in its own sprint.)*

### Act 1 — The bit
0s and 1s; counting in binary; the reveal that text, images, and sound are all
just numbers in disguise (encoding). Memory in a computer introduced as
numbered boxes holding these numbers.

### Act 2 — Bits that decide
True/false; Boolean algebra; logic gates (AND, OR, NOT, and the universal
ones); building an adder from gates. (Cross-path checkpoint: the physical gate
is a transistor — needs the ATOM path's moving charge, Act 3.)

### Act 3 — The machine
Memory as numbered boxes, so allocation; the CPU's fetch-execute heartbeat;
what a program actually is; system architecture.

### Act 4 — The tower of languages
Machine code, then assembly, then compilers and interpreters, then how Python
and Java actually run when you press enter.

### Act 5 — Machines talk
Packets, IP, DNS; what happens when you type a URL; how the internet works.

### Act 6 — Machines learn
Data; then statistics and probability (cross-path: the LINE path's counting and
chance); then machine-learning foundations.

## 4. THE UNIT — the smallest piece of measurement / description

### Act 1 — The seven words
1. **The regress** — define "speed"; every definition leans on another word, so
   a few must be left undefined on purpose.
2. **What must be chosen** — some quantities are irreducible; they can only be
   pointed at. The base units are that pointing.
3. **The seven** — metre, second, kilogram, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela —
   each justified as *why it cannot be built from the others*.
4. **The drift problem** — a unit must not change; the 2019 anchoring of every
   base unit to a constant of nature (second to caesium, metre to light).
5. **Theorems begin** — speed = length over time; area = length times length;
   every quantity is the seven in costume.
6. **The grammar of reality** — dimensional analysis as a grammar-check: an
   equation whose sides do not reduce to the same base words is meaningless.
7. **Force, predicted before felt** — mass times length over time-squared
   invents force before you meet Newton; the newton earns its name.

### Act 2 — From words to motion (the handoff)
8. **The first sentence** — length plus time gives speed; average vs instant
   planted (the seed of calculus).
9. **Change of change** — acceleration is length over time over time; nesting
   the words one level deeper.
10. **The confluence** — explicit handoff: everything built from words is what
    you need to describe a moving object; you now need a mass on a line. **The
    UNIT path merges into the LINE path at motion (its step 9/10).**

### The remaining units (returnable deep-dives, not a straight line)
Act 2 spent only two of the seven (metre, second). The other five each open a
domain and can be entered when that domain is reached:
- **The kilogram** — mass in two disguises (heaviness vs stubbornness); the last
  unit to lose its physical prototype (2019, Planck constant). The bridge unit:
  feeds LINE's F = ma and ATOM's gravity.
- **The ampere** — defined by the force between wires (a base unit defined via a
  force). Feeds the ATOM path's current, rigorously.
- **The kelvin** — temperature as average jiggle; absolute zero as a floor. Pays
  the ATOM path's "heat is jiggle" debt. Opens thermodynamics.
- **The mole** — a *count*, not a measurement; the chemist's bridge to the human
  hand (Avogadro's number, met in the ATOM path, now formalised).
- **The candela** — luminous intensity, defined partly by the human eye; the one
  base unit with a person built in. Opens toward light.
- **The closing** — all seven spent; the fan of derived units (newton, joule,
  watt, volt, pascal) each shown as its base-word recipe. The
  periodic-table-of-units.

The UNIT path is the riverbed: a short prequel that pours into the LINE path,
then a spine the user re-enters to spend each remaining unit where it does work.

## How the four relate

- **Four smallest things:** space/abstraction (line), matter (atom),
  information (bit), measurement (unit).
- **They cross; they do not funnel.** The designed crossings:
  - ATOM step 15 (charge has direction) uses LINE step 16 (the arrow).
  - BIT Act 2 (the gate as transistor) uses ATOM Act 3 (moving charge).
  - BIT Act 6 (machine learning) uses the LINE path's statistics/probability.
  - UNIT feeds LINE (metre/second into motion), UNIT feeds ATOM (ampere into
    current), and the kelvin pays ATOM's heat debt.
- Each crossing is a soft suggestion plus a payoff, never a gate.
- There is **no single destination.** Some flows happen to meet (statistics,
  matrices, and optimization near machine learning), but the paths are open
  systems that keep branching, not rivers to one sea.

## Build order

LINE first (`LINE-SPRINT-HANDOVER.md`), then ATOM, BIT, UNIT — each its own
sprint reusing the same floor/micro-check/exit shell, tokens, and data shape.
Build path two as a **sibling** of the Line, not a new app: only the content and
the interactive SVGs change.
