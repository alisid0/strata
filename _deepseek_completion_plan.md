Right now the 50-topic spine is ~14% built—coordinate geometry, kinematics, Newtonian mechanics, atomic structure are locked, and a handful of other topics have partial drafts. The hub and bridge graph tells us which moves give the most leverage: build the topics that **embed a hub concept** early, and later topics that radiate from that hub fall into place with far less effort. Below is a concrete, opinionated roadmap shaped by that graph—not a restatement of the default phase numbering.

## 1. Build order: overall phases

**Phase A — Foundational mathematical literacy (the proportional & orthogonal hubs)**  
*Topics 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 7*  

Arithmetic, algebra, equations are the bedrock of **proportional reasoning** and are currently empty. Trigonometry and vectors complete the **orthogonal decomposition** hub and unlock half the physics syllabus (mechanics, circular motion, oscillations, fields). Exponentials/logs feed the **exponential process** hub (radioactive decay, kinetics, cooling). Without these, the higher physics and chemistry topics can’t be taught with real mechanism—they become shallow storytelling.

**Phase B — Calculus core (the instantaneous‑rate‑of‑change hub)**  
*Topics 11, 12, 14*  

Limits (partial) and differentiation (empty) are the engine for **instantaneous rate of change**. Building them now turns kinematics from a collection of definitions into a connected idea, and makes later topics (SHM, waves, thermodynamics, kinetics) genuinely teachable. Integration follows naturally and is the last core piece of the high‑school calculus story.

**Phase C — Mechanics & energy completion (conservation, circular motion, oscillations, gravitation, waves)**  
*Topics 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27*  

Work/energy (partial) and momentum (empty) lock the **conservation** hub. Circular motion and gravitation complete the story newtonian mechanics started; oscillations and waves are the first big payoff of orthogonal decomposition + calculus. These six topics turn the existing kinematics/forces base into a coherent physics worldview.

**Phase D — Electricity & magnetism extension**  
*Topics 28 (already partial via EL5‑16), 29, 30, 33, 34*  

Electricity is in progress; completing it and then building electric fields, magnetism, induction, and AC gives the **field concept** hub real teeth and connects to the atomic structure already built.

**Phase E — Chemistry expansion**  
*Topics 41, 42, 44, 45, 46, 43 (complete), 47, 48, 49, 50*  

With atomic structure locked and bonding/structure the next logical step, build out the core chemistry sequence. Stoichiometry (partial) needs completion early here; thermochemistry and equilibrium lean on the proportional and conservation hubs already built.

**Phase F — Advanced topics (light touch)**  
*Topics 10, 15, 16, 19 (complete lightly), 35, 36, 37, 38*  

Complex numbers, ODEs, quantum, relativity, experimental methods. These are pre‑university but sit at the edge—they need an honest, shallow introduction, not a full multi‑tier treatment.

## 2. Depth: what gets deep treatment, what gets light

I’m drawing the line at whether a topic is **foundational for everything that follows** (deep) or **a specialism that rounds out a pre‑university education but doesn’t itself unlock a dozen other topics** (light).

### Deserve genuinely deep treatment (many tiers)

- 1 Arithmetic, 2 Algebra, 3 Equations — no later topic works without fluency here.
- 6 Trigonometry, 9 Vectors — the orthogonal decomposition hub; every mechanics and fields topic requires them.
- 11 Limits, 12 Differentiation, 14 Integration — the calculus spine; also the rate‑of‑change hub.
- 20 Kinematics, 21 Newtonian mechanics — already built, but future tiers can add calculus‑based depth.
- 22 Work/energy, 23 Momentum — the conservation hub; both need full quantitative treatment.
- 28 Electricity & circuits — already in progress; needs to reach Kirchhoff and Ohm deep.
- 39 Atomic structure — built; future tiers can go deeper (orbitals, spectra).
- 41 Chemical bonding, 43 Stoichiometry, 44 Thermochemistry, 45 Equilibrium — the core of pre‑university chemistry.

### Deserve a light intro‑level touch (1‑2 tiers, qualitative or with simple calculations only)

- 10 Mathematical proof & logic — best woven into other topics; a single “what is a proof” tier is enough.
- 15 Differential equations — only the idea `dy/dx = ky` and its solution; no analytic methods.
- 16 Complex numbers — the complex plane and Euler’s formula, enough to support AC phasors later.
- 19 Measurement & units — a scaling/dimensional‑analysis tier; no need for a full separate path.
- 24 Circular motion, 25 Gravitation, 26 Oscillations, 27 Waves — these need careful algebra/trig treatment, but pre‑university stops before differential equation solutions; so 2‑3 tiers each, not a multi‑tier deep dive.
- 30 Magnetism, 33 EM induction, 34 AC circuits — qualitative right‑hand rules, Faraday’s law, basic transformer; no complex impedance.
- 35 Quantum foundations — photons, photoelectric effect, wave‑particle duality; no Schrödinger equation.
- 36 Atomic & nuclear physics — already partial; one or two extra tiers on radioactivity and half‑life, no deep models.
- 37 Special relativity — time dilation and length contraction as a single “wonder” tier, purely qualitative.
- 38 Experimental physics — a tier on uncertainty, significant figures, error bars; not a separate lab course.
- 49 Organic fundamentals, 50 Analytical chemistry — functional groups and mass spectrometry/IR as recognition topics, not mechanistic synthesis.

## 3. Are any topics genuinely low‑priority or questionable?

Not “remove from the list” low‑priority, but three don’t deserve equal weight:

- **10 Mathematical proof & logic** — a pre‑university spine needs proof *as a thread*, not a standalone chunk. I’d merge it into algebra, geometry, and calculus, giving it maybe a single “what a proof is” card.
- **19 Measurement, units & dimensional analysis** — essential, but it can be a short anchor topic that every other physics BB refers back to, rather than a full path. It’s already partial; a single tier of 3‑4 cards is enough.
- **38 Experimental physics & uncertainty** — again vital, but best delivered as a few cards that every practical‑facing BB links to, not a deep standalone sequence.

For the truly advanced topics (35, 37, 38), the question is whether they belong at all in a “minimum pre‑university spine”. I think they do—because top‑end high‑school syllabuses (A‑level, AP) include them, and Strata’s promise is a complete spine—but they must stay light, or they’ll eat months of authoring for marginal gain.

## 4. The next 10 topics (in priority order)

When the current Electricity (EL5‑16) and Matrices (MX1‑15) drafts are locked, plus any remaining coordinate‑geometry tiers, build these next. The list is ordered to unlock the most hubs and bridges per topic built.

1. **Arithmetic fluency & estimation (1)** — unbuilt, no path.  
   *Leverage:* The proportional‑reasoning hub starts here. Without it, every later quantitative topic (stoichiometry, Ohm’s law, energy calculations) is blocked.  
   *Depth:* Deep. Many tiers of mental‑arithmetic, order‑of‑magnitude, and estimation BBs.

2. **Algebraic manipulation (2)** — unbuilt.  
   *Leverage:* The next proportional‑reasoning layer; needed to write and rearrange every formula from kinematics to equilibrium.  
   *Depth:* Deep.

3. **Equations & inequalities (3)** — unbuilt.  
   *Leverage:* Completes the algebra foundation; the “solve for x” skill that makes Ohm’s law, stoichiometry, and energy problems solvable.  
   *Depth:* Deep.

4. **Trigonometry (6)** — partial (Cards 17‑20,24 exist but not wired in a path).  
   *Leverage:* The orthogonal‑decomposition hub; sin/cos as component ratios. Without solid trig, circular motion, oscillations, vectors, and fields are surface‑only.  
   *Depth:* Deep. Build a full Tier 0‑1 path with worked examples.

5. **Vectors (9)** — partial (Cards 21‑23).  
   *Leverage:* Second pillar of orthogonal decomposition; also bridges coordinate‑geometry → forces (via components), and forces → fields. Completing it now makes SHM, fields, and matrices‑as‑transformations feel like a natural extension.  
   *Depth:* Deep. 2D thoroughly, then a light 3D extension.

6. **Limits & continuity (11)** — partial (Cards 12,13,15).  
   *Leverage:* The instantaneous‑rate‑of‑change hub; the “shrink the interval to zero” idea is the bridge from kinematics to differentiation. Five drafted bridge‑cards already exist—lock them into real BBs.  
   *Depth:* Deep.

7. **Exponentials & logarithms (7)** — unbuilt.  
   *Leverage:* The exponential‑process hub; unlocks half‑life, first‑order kinetics, Newton’s law of cooling. If you build this before nuclear physics and kinetics, you can teach those topics with a shared mathematical language instead of hand‑waving.  
   *Depth:* Deep. Start with growth/decay, then logs as inverse.

8. **Differentiation (12)** — unbuilt.  
   *Leverage:* Core calculus; the rate‑of‑change hub in its pure form. Makes kinematics, mechanics, and oscillations quantitative. Without it, “instantaneous speed” remains a vague notion.  
   *Depth:* Deep. Power rule, sum rule, chain rule, second derivative; all with worked numeric examples.

9. **Work, energy & power (22)** — partial (Cards 10,11).  
   *Leverage:* Conservation hub. Already partially built and directly bridges forces → electricity via the energy‑per‑coulomb idea. Completing it with quantitative tiers and the work‑energy theorem turns a sketch into a tool.  
   *Depth:* Deep. Finish the work/KE link, add power as a rate, and include spring potential.

10. **Momentum & collisions (23)** — unbuilt.  
    *Leverage:* Completes the conservation hub alongside energy; makes the “everything pulls” gravity card and the stopping‑distance cards genuinely predictive. The forces‑to‑momentum bridge is a single, high‑payoff edge.  
    *Depth:* Deep. Conservation of momentum, impulse, elastic/inelastic collisions with numeric examples.

After these ten, the remaining physics topics (circular motion, gravitation, oscillations, waves, thermo) can be built with far less friction, because the underlying hubs are already in place. That’s the real value of following the graph: each early build makes the next five faster, not just one.
