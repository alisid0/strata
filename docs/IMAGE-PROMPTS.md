# Image prompts — all 37 BBs

> **LEGACY STATIC-DECK PROMPTS.** These are not a live catalogue or current
> visual policy. Technical diagrams must be code-native; see
> `docs/SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md`.

Chalkboard illustrations, **~2 per BB**. Every prompt is grounded in what that BB actually teaches. Cards 1–11 already have images (listed here too, so you can regenerate to 1:1 if you want consistency); 12–37 are the priority — they have none.

## How to use
- Each prompt below already includes the house style. Paste it straight into the image tool.
- **Format: 1:1 square**, important content centered, generous empty chalkboard margin around the edges (so nothing important sits near an edge that might get letterboxed on phone vs desktop).
- A BB can use **more than one image** — where two are listed, image **a** is usually the layer-0 "scene," image **b** a deeper floor (a diagram or the formula).
- **Proof the text/equations** the generator bakes in — these tools garble letters and symbols. If an equation comes out wrong, leave it out of the image and let the BB's own dashed formula box carry it.

## The shared style (already baked into every prompt)
> Hand-drawn white chalk illustration on a dark green chalkboard (#1f3b30). Slightly rough, imperfect chalk lines with faint dust and smudging. Simple, clear, diagrammatic — a teacher's quick board sketch, not polished vector art. Off-white chalk for main lines; pale yellow (#f2d585) and pale green (#a9d6a0) chalk for emphasis and labels only. No colour photography, no realism, no gradients or soft shading. Flat educational diagram. Square 1:1 composition, subject centered, generous empty board space around the edges.

# ACT V-equivalent — Trigonometry, vectors & angles

## BB 17 — What trigonometry is
- **a.** Style as above. A chalk ship in distress at sea (no lights), a distant lighthouse or cliff on the horizon, a dashed sightline from ship to top of cliff making an angle with the water. Atmospheric but simple. "an angle hides a distance" feel.
- **b.** Style as above. A clean right-angled triangle with its three sides and one marked angle θ, small chalk note "angles ↔ sides". Centered, the foundational diagram. (Proof θ.)

## BB 18 — The parts of a right triangle
- **a.** Style as above. A large clear chalk right-angled triangle, the right-angle square corner marked, the angle θ marked at another corner, and the three sides labelled: hypotenuse (longest, opposite the right angle), opposite (across from θ), adjacent/base (beside θ). Labels in pale yellow/green, very legible. (Proof labels.)
- **b.** Style as above. The same triangle shown twice with θ in two different corners, showing how "opposite" and "base" swap depending on which angle you pick. Diagrammatic.

## BB 19 — Sine, cosine, tangent
- **a.** Style as above. A right triangle with the three sides colour-tagged (chalk white/yellow/green), and three small ratio boxes beside it: sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj. Clean, legible. (Proof the ratios carefully.)
- **b.** Style as above. A "SOH CAH TOA" chalk mnemonic laid out neatly, each triple under its name, with a tiny triangle icon. Centered, generous margin. (Proof letters.)

## BB 20 — Cracking the captain's problem
- **a.** Style as above. A chalk scene: a flagpole casting a shadow on the ground, a dashed sightline from shadow tip to flag top forming an angle, "a shadow hides an angle". Then the same idea aimed at a distant cliff/island across water. Centered.
- **b.** Style as above. A triangle overlaid on the cliff-and-distance scene: known angle, known base distance, solving for the unreachable height. "one angle, one distance, a fixed ratio" in pale green. Diagrammatic.

## BB 21 — How much isn't enough (vectors & scalars)
- **a.** Style as above. A chalk map/grid: a house at one corner, a playground elsewhere; a stepped path going right then up (two arrows head-to-tail) and a straight diagonal "bird's flight" arrow from house to playground. Labels in pale green.

## BB 22 — When two things push at once (resultants)

## BB 23 — Splitting a diagonal (resolving vectors)

## BB 24 — The made-up 360 (radians)

## BB 25 — The three accelerators

- **b.** Style as above. A single velocity arrow shown three ways: lengthened (throttle), shortened (brake), turned to a new direction (steering) — three small panels, each labelled, with a dashed box "a = Δv / Δt". (Proof equation.)

## BB 26 — The infinite tightrope (number line, 1D)

## BB 27 — The grid of reality (2D Cartesian)

## BB 28 — The two lives of a falling rock (axis independence)

## BB 29 — The perfect arc (projectile motion)

## BB 31 — Dash and the staircase (y = mx + c)

## Notes for the generator
- These tools reliably mangle longer text and equations. For any prompt marked "(Proof…)", check the output; if the symbols are wrong, regenerate, or drop the equation and let the BB's dashed formula box show it instead.
- Keep people as simple stick or line figures — consistent with the existing 11.
- If a board feels too busy as one image, use the **a/b split** as two separate images on two floors rather than cramming.
- Save as `images/card-NN.png` (or `card-NN-a.png` / `card-NN-b.png` if a BB uses two).
