# QUBIX-WRITING-STYLE.md — the voice of every BB

Merged from three sources:
1. **Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing** — the structural pattern catalog
2. **George Orwell, "Politics and the English Language"** — the timeless rules
3. **Paul Lockhart, "The Mending of Broken Bones"** — the mathematical voice

---

## The negative rules (what to strip out)

### Orwell's six rules (slightly adapted for BBs)

1. Never use a metaphor you've seen in print before.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut a word out, cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word, or jargon if you can think of
   an everyday English equivalent.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than write something that sounds fake.

### AI structural patterns — banned entirely

These are the Wikipedia-confirmed AI fingerprints. None of them appear in
Lockhart. None of them should appear in a Qubix BB.

| Pattern | Example (AI) | Why it's a tell |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Parallel negation | "Not X, but Y" / "It's not this — it's that" | AI loves rhetorical contrast |
| "No X. No Y. No Z." | "No width. No grain. No ends." | Speechwriter device, not prose |
| "Every X has Y" x3 | "Every mark has a start. Every crack has an edge." | AI catalogues; humans trust one |
| Three-act paragraph | Claim → develop → tidy landing | Humans end mid-thought |
| "And yet" pivot | Build tension → "And yet..." | AI editorializing |
| "Here is the thing" | "Here is the uncomfortable part." | AI performing insight |
| Em dash overuse | More than 2 per piece | Wikipedia: "the ChatGPT dash" |
| Tricolon | "Bridges. Planets. The moon." | AI sounds comprehensive |
| Summary endings | "Ultimately, what matters is..." | AI can't leave a thought hanging |

### AI vocabulary — never use

Tier 1 (strongest signals): delve, tapestry, landscape, pivotal, underscore,
testament, intricate, meticulous, nuanced, multifaceted, embark, spearhead,
bolster, garner, interplay, realm, labyrinth, symphony.

Tier 2 (moderate): crucial, vibrant, foster, enhance, leverage, navigate,
resonate, illuminate, showcase, enduring, robust, holistic, comprehensive,
innovative, dynamic, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer.

Tier 3 (transitions): furthermore, moreover, additionally, consequently,
nevertheless, subsequently, notably, indeed, nonetheless, hence, thus.

## The positive rules (what to do instead)

### Lockhart's moves

1. **Ask genuine questions and work through them.** Not rhetorical setups.
   > "How weird can a number system get and still obey our nine demands?"

2. **Use "we" naturally.**
   > "So we have opened the barn door fairly wide..."

3. **Acknowledge limits.**
   > "It is certainly not my intention to go into great detail here, but..."

4. **Use humor.**
   > "much to their surprise and delight (not to mention mystification and horror)"

5. **Vary sentence length deliberately.** Put a 3-word sentence next to a
   25-word one.

6. **Trust the reader.** State the fact. Don't tell them it's important.

7. **Casual connectors only.** "So," "Well," "As it happens," "Of course."
   Not "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Consequently."

8. **End abruptly sometimes.** Let a paragraph stop without a landing.

9. **One concrete thing at a time.** Pick the strongest example and trust it.

10. **Write like you're at a blackboard with one person.**

### Orwell's positive guidance

> "What is above all needed is to let the meaning choose the word, and not the
> other way about. In prose, the worst thing one can do with words is to
> surrender to them."

> "Probably it is better to put off using words as long as possible and get
> one's meanings as clear as one can through pictures and sensations. Afterward
> one can choose — not simply accept — the phrases that will best cover the
> meaning."

## The BB writing process

### Before writing a single word

1. What is the ONE thing this BB teaches? Write it in one sentence.
2. What concrete thing does the reader already know that points toward it?
   (Sand groove → line. Pencil mark → line. Crack → line.)
3. What is the tension? (The everyday meaning and Euclid's meaning are
   different. That's interesting.)

### Drafting

4. Write Floor 1 without looking at any reference. Just tell the person next to
   you what you want them to understand.
5. Read it aloud. If you stumble, rewrite. If it sounds rehearsed, rewrite.
6. Run the checklist below.

### Checklist (every floor, every time)

```
[ ] No sentence starts with "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally"
[ ] No "Not X, but Y" / "No X. No Y. No Z." / "Every X has Y" patterns
[ ] No sentence tells the reader how to feel
[ ] No paragraph ends with a tweetable summary
[ ] No AI vocabulary words (Tiers 1-3)
[ ] Fewer than 2 em dashes in the entire BB
[ ] At least one sentence under 5 words
[ ] At least one sentence over 20 words
[ ] At least one paragraph that ends abruptly
[ ] Can be read aloud without sounding like a TED talk
```

### After drafting

7. Delete the first sentence of every floor. It's usually throat-clearing.
8. Delete the last sentence of every floor. It's usually editorializing.
9. Read the whole BB aloud in one go. If any floor sounds like a different
   person wrote it, rewrite that floor.
